The attacks that target people, not computers

Social engineering is when an attacker manipulates a person into giving up information, money, or access. Instead of breaking through a firewall, they get someone to open the door for them.

What is social engineering?

Most security tools are built to stop technical attacks like malware and hacked servers. Social engineering skips all of that. The attacker pretends to be someone you trust, like a coworker, your bank, IT support, or your boss, and uses pressure or friendliness to get you to act before you think.[3]

It works because it uses normal human instincts. We want to help people, we respect authority, and we react fast when something sounds urgent. Attackers count on all three.

Three attacks we cover

Each one is its own page. They overlap a lot: BEC is basically pretexting done over email, and phishing is often how attackers get the account access BEC needs.

Pretexting

Inventing a story to get what you want

The attacker builds a fake scenario, like an IT tech fixing an "urgent issue" or an auditor on a site visit, so their request seems legit.
